The look-alike models where the Confusion-species numbers come from — Match (BioCLIP 2 + the misID model) and Sound (BirdNET+ V3.0)

Match — the misID model, built on BioCLIP 2

BioCLIP 2 (Imageomics Institute, 2025) is a CLIP-style vision–language model trained on TreeOfLife-200M, about 214 million organism photos paired with their taxonomic names. Its text tower turns a scientific name into an embedding of 768 numbers that lies close to the embeddings of that species’ photos, so two names whose vectors point the same way belong to birds that look alike in pictures. BirdsWhere uses only the published name embeddings, no images: 11,131 bird names, 9,610 matched to the app’s species. The cosine similarity of two names and each name’s rank among the other’s nearest are the strongest inputs of the misID model, a gradient-boosted classifier (HistGradientBoosting, monotone constraints) trained on about 803,000 ordered species pairs to predict whether a partner holds at least 5 % of a species’ real iNaturalist misidentifications at equal exposure. Its other inputs are AVONET shape, size and mass, ecological niche and lifestyle, genus / family / order and HBW plumage colour. Where and when the birds live is deliberately not an input, so the model can only learn appearance; its probability × 100 is the app’s Match. Geography enters the app once, as Here.

Among candidates that actually share the species’ range and season it finds 92 % of the real confusion partners in its top 10 (3,893 species), and it keeps 60 % of the look-alikes that never meet in the top 10, where the geography-aware model keeps 44 % — that is why geography stays out. The misID column beside Match is the real iNaturalist share itself, corrected for how often the two birds are in the same place at the same time of year.

Sound — BirdNET+ V3.0 over Xeno-canto (Experimental features)

BirdNET+ V3.0 (Cornell Lab of Ornithology and Chemnitz University of Technology, developer preview 3.1, 2026, CC BY-SA 4.0) is a bird-sound classifier with 11,560 labels — 9,834 birds plus some insects, amphibians and mammals — that also gives a 1,280-number embedding for every 5-second window of audio. 44,544 Xeno-canto recordings of 9,021 species (A grade, B grade where A ran out; the first 45 s of each) were cut into 297,859 windows; a window counts when the model gives the labelled species at least 20 % (159,025 kept, 53 %). Each species gets the centroid of its kept embeddings and its soft confusion — the mean probability the model puts on every other species on windows where the label is on top. A pair’s closeness is the rank-average of centroid similarity and soft confusion, stored for each bird’s eight nearest as a 0–100 score: the app’s Sound. Google’s Perch 2.0 was run on the same recordings; it recognised fewer species (5,027 against 7,475 with a usable entry) and was 2 points more accurate on the 4,996 species both cover (78.5 % against 76.1 % held-out), so BirdNET+ was chosen for its coverage. Hover a map — or tap it — for the cell’s values in both models and their difference (a tap pins the readout; tap again to release). Both models take raw latitude, longitude and week (1–48) and return a per-species occurrence probability (sigmoid). The grid covers 25° W – 45° E, 34° N – 72° N at the chosen step; every cell is one model call per model, run on this device with ONNX Runtime Web. Species are matched between the two label files by their species code (11,880 shared; 132 only in V3.0.0, 2,202 only in V3.0.4), with the scientific name as the fallback.
